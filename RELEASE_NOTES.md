# What's new in Moatt

## October 17, 2026

**Clearer updates about Moatt**

Future updates will explain what has changed in the clothing design experience and why it matters, using words anyone can understand.

This update changes how future work is described. It does not change the clothing design tools themselves.

**Better decisions for future clothing design improvements**

Future changes can draw on trusted design research and compare different ways to make the editor easier to use. We'll check the results before treating a new approach as an improvement.

The clothing design tools themselves have not changed as part of this update.

**Ask for design improvements in ordinary words**

You can describe what you want to improve without choosing special settings first. The assistant has guidance to consider the existing clothing design experience, compare useful alternatives and explain its recommendations clearly.

The clothing design tools themselves have not changed.

## October 27, 2026 — Design concepts and supplier conversations

**Start designing from a written idea**

Describe a hoodie or T-shirt, its color, fit, fabric appearance and optional wording. Moatt can turn the parts it recognizes into an editable starting point. It keeps your existing artwork and you can undo the change. It does not generate clothing images or understand every request.

**Bring a clear visual brief to a manufacturer**

You can save a document showing the front and back designs, selected garment options and questions to settle with a clothing maker. This is for discussion only. It does not contain verified measurements, sewing patterns or manufacturing instructions.

These improvements are committed for review, but are not confirmed live on a published website.

**Keep a small collection of designs**

Save several designs in your browser, open an earlier one, make a new design, or copy an existing design to try another color. Download backup files if you want to keep your work safe across devices.

**More clothing details and brand notes**

Choose a visual pocket style, save your brand name and add supplier questions. The 3D design previews now have gently rounded surfaces and corrected artwork direction on the back.

The mannequin and clothing remain illustrations, not proof of fit or manufacturing-ready garments.


## October 27, 2026 — Clearer clothing and mannequin previews

**A smoother mannequin**

Moatt now opens with a simple, smooth display mannequin instead of the visibly blocky character. You can still switch to the older character to compare.

**Clothes look less flat**

T-shirt and hoodie outlines now have softer shoulders and edges. We've added gentle folds and finishing details, improved the studio lighting, and refined the look of the clothing around the mannequin.

**Fabrics have different surface character**

Cotton has a fine, soft weave; denim has stronger diagonal texture; satin has a smoother surface with brighter highlights. The artwork editor and 3D preview share the same visual fabric choice, while exported print artwork remains transparent.

These changes improve the *appearance* of your designs, but they do not provide true cloth simulation, exact sizes or factory-approved production files. They are not published to the live website yet.


## October 28, 2026 — More natural clothing previews

**Clothing now has a rounded body**

The T-shirt and hoodie previews use rounded front and back surfaces rather than a flat cutout. The sleeves extend around the mannequin's arms, and the neckline and hems have their own shapes. Your front and back designs remain editable separately.

**The display mannequin looks less segmented**

The studio mannequin now has continuous arms, legs, torso and head. The older licensed mannequin remains available as a comparison.

These changes are being reviewed and are not confirmed live. The clothes are still an illustrated preview, not a simulation of real fabric movement, verified body measurements or factory-ready sewing patterns.


**The sleeves no longer show mannequin shoulders through the fabric**

The mannequin now shows its forearms below short sleeves and only the hands near long sleeves. This prevents the mannequin from sticking out through the upper part of the garment in the preview.

## October 28, 2026 — A presentation you can actually share

**Front and back on one clean design board**

There is now a **Presentation** view beside the drawing and three-dimensional views. It shows both sides of your selected shirt or hoodie on an editorial-style design sheet with your actual artwork, selected color, and fabric appearance. You can save it as a high-resolution PNG and share it without opening the three-dimensional mannequin.

**Useful even when 3D isn't supported**

The presentation works through the normal browser canvas. You can still edit and reopen the original project separately. Nothing about this export changes or deletes your design.

The presentation is a concept image to help you show an idea. It does not certify real fabric folds, fit, or print measurements. These changes are in review and are not confirmed live on the published website.

**Better starting clothing choices**

Choosing a hoodie now starts with long sleeves and a front pouch, rather than a short-sleeve shirt with a hood. Choosing a T-shirt starts with short sleeves. You can still change these details afterward, and requesting a short-sleeve hoodie or no pocket explicitly is respected. Text from a written idea is slightly larger and easier to see on the design sheet.

**Better on phones**

You can now enlarge the presentation to fill the screen, close it again, and use the top download button to save the entire design sheet. This works without changing the project or losing your original artwork.

## October 30, 2026 — Start designing without a blank canvas

**Three ready-to-edit ways to begin**

Choose a small chest mark, a bold statement, or a matching front-and-back brand layout. Moatt adds editable wording to your current design without deleting what you already made. You can change the words and use Undo if you prefer your previous version.

**Position your artwork without guessing coordinates**

Move selected artwork to the top, center or bottom of the suggested design area, or fit an oversized design inside it. You can also copy a design element to the other side of the garment and edit each copy separately.

These guides are for arranging a visual idea. They are not verified printing sizes or positions from a manufacturer. On phones, quick-start choices are now accessible near the design canvas, and controls are kept out of the way of the artwork. This work is in review and is not confirmed available on a published site.

## October 30, 2026 — Give clothing designs a stronger visual identity

**Shape the words as well as the garment**

Choose a bold display look, an editorial serif, a clean typeface or a utility-style label. Adjust letter spacing, line spacing, alignment and boldness. Long slogans automatically wrap and reduce in size when they fit the available space.

**Use styles without rebuilding your design**

Try ready-made streetwear, editorial and utility typography treatments. Changes are editable, can be undone and stay with the project when you reopen it. The same text design is used in the artwork and presentation exports.

These are local font choices rather than purchased typefaces. Very long wording in a tiny box may still need a larger box. These changes are in review and have not been confirmed live.
